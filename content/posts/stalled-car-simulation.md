In a [previous post](/posts/automotive-nannyware/), I moaned about how the entire [Subaru](https://www.subaru.com) product line has added engine Automatic Stop/Start hardware (aka ASS) to all of its vehicles in the 2026 models. This is apparently old news, as the technology has been around since 2012 or thereabouts, and I have been fortunate in avoiding the "feature" until now.

I was in a parking lot the other day, and a car near me came to a stop and the engine went silent. I thought, "Uh oh, did the car stall out for some reason?", but no, the driver pressed on the accelerator, and I could hear the car start up again. Lovely.

It turns out ASS has never been mandatory, rather manufacturers were bribed into adding it to be eligable for some kind of financial credit, and to improve overall [CAFE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_average_fuel_economy) numbers.

The Trump administration has proposed doing away with ASS compliance requirements, and that's a good thing. Details in [this article](https://www.ericpetersautos.com/2026/02/14/stop-it-please/)

One big obstacle to seeing this crap go away is "regulatory uncertainty", aka what one administration taketh away a subsequent administration can putteth back. I get that this is a serious consideration, and it's discussed a bit in [this article](https://www.thetruthaboutcars.com/cars/news-blog/is-auto-stop-start-actually-going-away-45133921)

Actual feedback from manufacturers were outlined in [Car And Driver](https://www.caranddriver.com/news/a70350799/epa-eliminating-stop-start-automaker-comments/) - lotsa weasel words...

We won't need to make any vehicle purchase decisions until Q4 of 2027, and a lot of things can change by then. Maybe some manufacturers will go to offering ASS as an add-on option on some models, for example. For now though, ASS is a show-stopper for a new purchase or a lease.

As in kiss my ASS.
