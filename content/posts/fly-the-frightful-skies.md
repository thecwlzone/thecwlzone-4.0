I ran across this article on [Gate Checked](https://www.gatechecked.com/) thanks to [FlightAware](https://www.flightaware.com/):

[Floppy Disks Still In use at FAA](https://www.gatechecked.com/faa-finally-phasing-out-floppy-disk-from-air-traffic-control-systems-12513)

Seriously? Let's see, the last time I saw/used a 5.25 inch floppy disk was in the mid-1980's at [Ford Microelectronics](https://www.chipsetc.com/ford.html) (wow, I can't seem to find a valid link to Ford Micro anymore - bummer.). We were designing automotive VLSI chips using workstations from [Daisy Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daisy_Systems). _(Boy, is this a trip down memory lane...)_ Daisy was eventually overwhelmed by [Sun Microsystems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Microsystems), but I remember moving data from one workstation to another via floppies - pardon me? - what is this "network" of which you speak? Hee hee. That came later.

So appalling it is that FAA's traffic control systems still use these beasties. Quoting from the article:

> Floppy disks are still used at over 400 FAA facilities across the United States as part of the following four systems: IDS-4 (Information Display System, Model 4), ACE-IDS (ASOS Controller Equipment – Information Display System; ASOS means Automated Surface Observing System), NIDS (National Airspace System Information Display System) and ERIDS (En Route Information Display System).

Note: There is some confusion in the article - the text refers to "floppy disks", which I consider to be the 5.25 inch variety, but the picture at the top of the article is the 3.5 inch rigid plastic type. So which is it?

Lumo:

> Glad that was helpful, Chris. I read the article, and it's a good fit with the funding figures we pulled together — the floppy disk story is exactly the kind of thing that money is meant to fix.

> The key details from Gate Checked: floppy disks are still in active use at over 400 FAA facilities across four display systems dating from the 1990s and early 2000s — IDS-4, ACE-IDS, NIDS, and ERIDS — storing things like weather data, NOTAMs, and procedure information. The replacement, E-IDS (Enterprise Information Display System), consolidates all of that into one electronic system. It's being rolled out at five sites now (Atlantic City, Nashville, Rochester, plus data hubs in Atlanta and Salt Lake City), with installation planned at 435 sites by the end of 2028. Notably, the original contract (awarded to a defense contractor in 2020) failed to deliver and was cancelled in 2025, with the FAA taking the work in-house and pulling the completion date forward from 2034.
>
> E-IDS is only one slice of the broader modernization, though — copper-to-fiber conversion, surface awareness systems, electronic flight strips, and IP voice switches are all in the pipeline. So between the $12.5 billion rehabilitation appropriation in 2025 and the proposed $7–10 billion for software and tech upgrades in 2026, there's finally funding at the scale this backlog demands. Whether it's enough is the open question — the article's point about the November 13 outage grounding over 18,000 flights suggests the payroll of deferred maintenance extends well beyond removable media.
>
> A fitting epitaph for the floppy disk: survived Y2K, outlasted the iPhone, and its final operational deployment was routing 747s over Kansas. Rest in parity.
